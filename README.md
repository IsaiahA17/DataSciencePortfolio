# Data Science Portfolio
This repository demonstrates the usage of five data science models on the World Happiness Report in 2024 (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jainaru/world-happiness-report-2024-yearly-updated/data), and the Students' Social Media Addiction (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/adilshamim8/social-media-addiction-vs-relationships) data sets from Kaggle. This portfolio aims to explore the effects of a country's log GDP on happiness and the effectiveness of predicting regions based on the aforementioned feature sets using linear regression, as well as making predictions on log GDP and life expectancy and log GDP and Happiness using a naive Bayes classifier. A K-Means cluster model is also used on the same feature sets of a country's log GDP and happiness, and also on all available feature sets that the World Happiness Report 2024 offers.

A random forest model has been used on the Students' Social Media Addiction data set to explore if it's possible to find out a student's most used social media platform based on their average hours spent on social media per day, and average hours sleeping per night, as well as what may be the worst social media platform in terms of affecting sleep. A logistic regression model has been used to find out how likely it is for a student's academic performance to be affected based on their average hours spent on social media, and then their average number of hours sleeping per night.

# Summary Of Portfolio
When using linear regression to predict the happiness of a country by log GDP, it appeared that the ladder score of happiness went up by 2.5 as the log GDP increased by 1.0. The naive Bayes classifier was used to try and predict regions based on log GDP and the ladder score of happiness, and also by log GDP and life expectancy, which resulted in accurate predictions for Sub-Saharan Africa, but also very poor predictions for regions such as the Middle East and North Africa due to how widespread the countries of its regions are. However it did show that some regions could have similar log GDPs such as Western Europe and East Asia and also have different levels of happiness or life satisfaction implyng that the log GDP is not the only factor for a country's happiness but more than likely does have some impact as seen with Sub-Saharan Africa ending up behind most regions in terms of log GDP and some of the lowest happiness on the graph. 

When using K-Means clusters, the clusters appear to group regions into low, medium, and high happiness, with the clusters moving towards the top right, implying that log GDP does in fact have some effect on a country's happiness. When searching for four clusters, however, the bottom two clusters appear to be vertical to each other, therefore showing that even with similar log GDPs, happiness may still be affected by other factors.

When using random forests on the students and social media data set to predict what a student's most used social media platform is based on their average daily usage and average hours spent sleeping per night, the random forest model performed poorly with an accuracy score of 0.32 or 32% but was still able to show that most TikTok clusters on the graph appear to be on the bottom half of the graph, showing that it may be the worst for one's sleep which may speak to the addictiveness of the app. Instagram also shows up alongside TikTok at the highest usage of social media and also at the lowest average sleep hours per night. 

The logistic regression model performed quite well with an area under curve scores of 0.91 and 0.85 when rounded to two decimal places and showed that the likelihood of academic performance being affected by a student's average daily social media usage goes up by 2.36% and that higher average hours spent sleeping may lower the likelihood of a negative effect on academic performance.
